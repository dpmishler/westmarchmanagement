Practice

Four kinds of work. One standard of proof.

Every engagement ends with something running in production and a paper trail showing it was tested against live systems — not a slide deck about potential.

S.01

Process survey

Two to three weeks inside how the work actually happens — orders, back office, scheduling, the spreadsheet nobody admits to. The deliverable is a costed map: where AI pays, where it doesn't, and what each crossing costs to build.

S.02

Integration engineering

Wiring models and automation into the systems you already run — point-of-sale, CRM, accounting, communications. Contracts are verified against the live system before launch, because vendor documentation lies and your revenue shouldn't find that out first.

S.03

Platform builds

When the road doesn't exist yet: customer-facing apps, operator portals, data pipelines. Built in phases that each ship working software, with the payment paths and failure cases engineered first, not last.

S.04

Operations & reliability

The unglamorous part that makes the rest true: evaluations, monitoring, reconciliation sweeps, decline handling. We stay on to hold the line — a flat monthly engagement, no surprise invoices.

Method

Survey. Build the road. Hold the line.

The sequence is the point: nothing gets built before the ground is understood, and nothing gets abandoned after it ships.

01

Survey the territory

We map your processes against what current AI can reliably do — in writing, with costs. If the honest answer is “AI doesn't pay here yet,” that's the report you get.

02

Build the road

Phased delivery, working software at every milestone. Money paths get engineered for the failure cases — retries, declines, duplicate charges — before any interface gets its polish.

03

Hold the line

Live verification before launch, then ongoing operation: monitoring, reconciliation, and honest reporting when something needs attention — delivered by the person who built it.

Fieldwork

A multi-location restaurant group, wired end to end.

SECTOR: QUICK-SERVICE RESTAURANTS · SCOPE: ORDERING PLATFORM · STATUS: IN DELIVERY

A Detroit-area restaurant group needed what the big chains have — a branded ordering app, loyalty, direct customer relationships — without a big chain's IT department. The territory: a point-of-sale system that had to remain the source of truth, payment flows where a mistake means charging someone twice, and a menu that changes weekly.

We surveyed first: every assumption about the POS vendor's API was encoded as an executable checklist and run against their live sandbox. The survey caught three integration defects in the vendor's documented behavior — including a payment-response format that would have marked every successful charge as a failure — before a single customer was exposed to any of them.

The build ships in phases, each independently verified: the pricing and loyalty engine, the POS integration, the hardened ordering API, and now the customer app. Every phase passed an adversarial review before the next began.
